# Tubby's Town skill map: Maths, English, EVS/Science, KG to Class 7


**Curriculum anchors**
- **Learning Outcomes at the Elementary Stage (2017):** https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/publication/otherpublications/tilops101.pdf. CBSE made it mandatory from 2019-20: https://www.cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2019/05_Circular_2019.pdf
- **Foundational stage (KG to Class 2):** NCERT's foundational-stage outcomes, https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/publication/otherpublications/Learning_Outcome_for_the_Foundational_Stage.pdf
- **Books:**
  - **Classes 1–2:** Joyful Mathematics (JM) and Mridang (MR). There is no separate EVS book at this stage; "the world around us" is woven into the language and maths books.
  - **Classes 3–5:** Maths Mela (MM), Santoor (SN) and Our Wondrous World (OWW). NCERT's 2025 price list shows these titles for Classes IV and V: https://ncertbooks.ncert.gov.in/static/Latest%20rate%202025.xlsx.pdf. Some Class 4 schools are still moving off the old Math-Magic, Marigold and Looking Around books.
  - **Classes 6–7:** Ganita Prakash (GP), Poorvi (PV) and Curiosity (CU). The GP 7 Part 2 chapter list is at https://teachoo.com/subjects/cbse-maths/class-7 and the CU 7 list at https://www.teachoo.com/subjects/science/class-7
- **Practice-format references:** IXL grade lists (https://www.ixl.com/ela/grade-2/skills and the matching maths pages), Khan Academy Kids, Prodigy.

**How to read the skill lists**
- **Codes:** S, M and C are the simple, medium and complex tiers. **G** means generated purely from rules. **G[x]** means generated from data table x (defined in section 3). **H** means items must be hand-written. **[!]** means the skill has a problem on a TV remote or with multiple choice.
- **"(have)"** marks your existing Maths skills.
- **Distractors:** every generated item should use near-miss distractors, such as off-by-one, a wrong operation or a common misconception, rather than random wrong answers.

## 2. Remote-friendly quiz formats (D-pad + OK)

| # | Format | Remote mechanics | Suits | Build |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | MCQ 2–4 (text) | ←→↑↓ focus, OK | everything | done |
| 2 | Picture pick (2×2 grid) | grid focus, OK | KG–2, vocabulary, EVS | Low |
| 3 | True/False | ← True, → False | facts, grammar check | Low |
| 4 | Odd one out | 4 tiles, OK | categories, shapes, POS | Low |
| 5 | Two baskets | each item drops in; ← or → sorts it; a run of 6–10 items | living/non-living, noun/verb, acid/base, wet/dry | Low–Med |
| 6 | Compare > < = | 3 buttons between two cards | numbers, fractions, decimals, weights | Low |
| 7 | Fill the gap | slot plus 3–4 letter or number tiles | missing letter, missing number, articles | Low |
| 8 | **Dial input** (combination lock) | ↑↓ changes a digit, ←→ moves between digits, OK submits. Gives open answers without typing. | any numeric answer, harder tiers | Med |
| 9 | Number-line hop | ←→ moves a frog, OK lands | integers, fractions, decimals, rounding | Med |
| 10 | Put in order | press tiles in sequence; each gets a number badge; Back undoes | ordering numbers, life cycles, sentences, stories | Med |
| 11 | Match pairs | pick a left item then a right item; a line draws; 3–4 pairs | antonyms, animal↔young, state↔capital | Med |
| 12 | Spot the mistake | sentence or sum split into tiles; OK on the wrong one | grammar, punctuation, worked-solution errors | Med |
| 13 | Clock set | ↑↓ hour hand, ←→ minute hand in 5- or 15-minute steps | time | Med |
| 14 | Grid navigate | D-pad moves a character on a map, "go 2 north" | directions, coordinates | Med |
| 15 | Story chain | 2–3 linked MCQs on one story ("first find…, then…") | complex tiers of word problems | Med |
| 16 | Listen and pick | Android TTS reads the prompt | pre-readers: initial sounds, sight words | Low (TTS), but voice quality varies |

The heaviest build is the renderers: angles, isometric cubes, circuits and nets.

**Biggest payoff:** LEX plus PIC plus five tables (IRR, ANIM, FOOD, OBJ, SCI scenarios) unlock roughly 70% of the non-maths skills. Maths needs only ctx.

## 4. Pitfalls and automatic validation

**Accuracy risks**
- **Antonyms and synonyms depend on sense.** light means dark or heavy; hot→cold but not "cool". Store an antonym only on a sense id. A distractor must not be a WordNet synonym or antonym of the target in **any** sense.
- **Ambiguous categories.** Tomato and cucumber (fruit or vegetable?), bat (mammal, though it flies), whale and dolphin (not fish), spider (not an insect), penguin (bird), wool (sheep, goat, yak). Keep a `contested` flag and exclude contested rows from category questions.
- **Spelling.** Use British spelling: colour, centre, metre, -ise, programme, mum, maths, "full stop". Lint every string with en_GB hunspell plus VarCon.
- **Indian conventions.**
  - Number grouping is 1,00,000; use lakh and crore.
  - Use ₹ with only coins and notes in circulation: coins ₹1/2/5/10/20; notes ₹10–500. The ₹2000 note was withdrawn in 2023.
  - Dates are DD/MM; units are metric.
  - Use the six Indian seasons, or summer/monsoon/winter.
- **Facts that change.** India has 28 states and 8 UTs since the 2020 Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu merger. Pluto status, record holders and state capitals also change. Avoid questions like "largest/most" unless the data source is pinned.
- **Regional variation.** Foods and houses belong to several states, and bin colours differ by city. Ask "which can be…", not "which is from…".
- **Maths generators.**
  - Distractors must not equal the answer (2+2 vs 2×2).
  - "Biggest" items must not have ties; equivalent fractions should not appear as separate options.
  - Use exact decimal or rational arithmetic, never floats.
  - Times need am/pm context.
  - Make percentages round.
- **Grammar ambiguity.** Prepositions, modals, articles with abstract nouns, and POS tagging (run as a noun) can have more than one right answer. Use curated frames, not free generation.
- **Bias and sensitivity.** Keep helper and pronoun pairings free of gender stereotypes. Adolescence and health items are hand-written and reviewed.

**Validation pipeline**
1. **Solver re-check.** Every generator emits the item plus a machine-checkable answer. An independent solver scores all options, and the item is rejected unless exactly one option is correct.
2. **Lexicon tests.** Antonyms must be symmetric and disjoint from synonyms. Inflections are checked against UniMorph. The rhyme key is checked against CMUdict. Every word in a prompt must be at or below the class level in LEX, as a readability gate.
3. **Facts.** Store at least 2 sources per table row (NCERT page plus Wikidata QID), and run a CI diff against a Wikidata SPARQL snapshot.
4. **Spelling.** Run an en_GB spell-lint plus a US-variant blocklist on all generated text.
5. **Ambiguity review.** Run an LLM judge over a sample, asking "could any other option be argued correct?", then human-review every flagged item and 2% of the rest.
6. **Seeded snapshot tests.** Keep these per generator for determinism and deduplication.
7. **In-field telemetry.** Flag items where one distractor draws more than 40% of picks, or where strong players fail, and quarantine them automatically.
